Layered surge protection

A whole-property protective device is intended to limit certain transient overvoltages at the electrical service or distribution equipment. It complements—not replaces—proper grounding, bonding, correctly installed branch-circuit protection, and point-of-use protection where appropriate. No surge device can guarantee protection from every event.

Generator-system electrical planning

A generator project requires more than choosing generator output. Planning may involve the loads that need backup, starting demand, distribution equipment, transfer equipment, fuel-system coordination, equipment location, ventilation, noise, and applicable requirements. Adelpro evaluates the electrical scope so the proposed system fits the property and intended use.

  • Identify essential and optional backup loads.
  • Review available service and distribution capacity.
  • Plan transfer and connection equipment for the selected system.
  • Test operation and explain safe system use after completion.

Good questions to prepare

Protection and backup-power questions

Does whole-property surge protection stop every surge?

No. It can reduce exposure to certain transient overvoltages, but performance depends on the event, installation, electrical system, grounding and bonding, and protective-device ratings.

How is generator size determined?

The required capacity depends on the loads to be supported, their starting characteristics, operating priorities, and the proposed equipment. A site-specific evaluation is needed.

Can one system serve a business?

Commercial and industrial backup requirements vary widely. Adelpro evaluates the property, equipment, operational priorities, and electrical distribution before defining a scope.
